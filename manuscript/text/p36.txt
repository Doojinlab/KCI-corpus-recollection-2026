Current Status and Research Gaps in AI-Assisted Chinese Language Education:
A Bibliometric and Network Comparison with English and Korean
Hong, Gildong
This study compared AI-assisted language education research in English, Korean, and Chinese
within a single analytical framework. Records indexed in the Korea Citation Index (KCI) from 2020
to June 2026 were collected through the KCI Open API and refined by a double-filtering procedure
—keyword coverage verification and abstract-based re-screening—yielding a corpus of 1,142 articles
identified under these search conditions (667 English, 421 Korean, 54 Chinese). Uniform
classification criteria were applied to build an 88-cell task-by-method grid, supplemented by trend,
topic-emergence, co-authorship, and citation analyses. Chinese-language studies accounted for 4.7%
of the corpus; within this subcorpus, 61.1% were single-authored and development and
performance-evaluation studies constituted 59.3%, whereas only one study experimentally compared
learning outcomes. Grid occupancy was 72.7% for English, 62.5% for Korean, and 27.3% for
Chinese: unlike English, concentrated in productive skills, and Korean, concentrated in development
and assessment, the small Chinese corpus was dispersed across many cells without accumulating
replications. Diffusion-pending coordinates—dense in the comparison groups but with zero or one
Chinese study—clustered in writing, speaking, and assessment, while 18 of the 88 cells were
structural gaps unobserved in all three domains, concentrated in listening and literature/culture. The
aim is not to rank the three domains but to distinguish coordinates where existing designs can be
selectively validated from those where new designs suited to Chinese must be developed.
Key Words: AI-Assisted Language Education, Chinese Language Education, Bibliometric
Analysis, Keyword Network Analysis, Research Gaps
